**Thumbnail-judging methodology** (run every thumbnail against this)

Mirror of the page, cover, and carousel judges. Ten sections, same gate logic — any unchecked box means the thumbnail does not ship. Written against elite, data-backed YouTube practices (vidIQ breakout studies, 500+ portfolio analyses, MrBeast-level patterns, CTR lifts of 20–60% from faces/contrast/text discipline, mobile-first realities where >70% of views occur at ~120–180 px width).

**Spec this was written against:** YouTube custom thumbnail — 1280 × 720 (16:9 landscape minimum; 1920 × 1080 preferred), ≤ 2 MB (desktop upload up to 50 MB still compresses), JPG or PNG, sRGB. Goal: stop the scroll **and** convert to click (CTR) while creating a curiosity gap that the title + video must fulfill. B2B / SaaS / creator / educational niches included; principles hold across niches. Two-to-three visual elements maximum; high-contrast system only.

**Screen / feed edition:** every print-object gate is N/A. Every composition, contrast, text, hierarchy, mobile-legibility, and curiosity-gap gate is enforced at **full size, at feed thumbnail size (~160–240 px wide), and under the 1-second / 120 px squint test**.

### 1. Format & technical readiness (8 gates)
- [ ] Dimensions are exactly 16:9 and ≥ 1280 × 720 (1920 × 1080 preferred); no letterboxing, no mixed ratios.
- [ ] File size ≤ 2 MB after export (or platform limit); no visible compression artifacts on faces or text at 2× zoom.
- [ ] No critical content in the bottom-right safe zone (YouTube duration stamp overlays it) or extreme bottom-left (chapter markers).
- [ ] Color space is sRGB; no CMYK or print profiles.
- [ ] Export is sharp at native size with no upscaling artifacts.
- [ ] Filename follows a stable convention (`brand-topic-YYYYMMDD-thumb.png`); never “final-v3.jpg”.
- [ ] The thumbnail is a single raster image (no multi-page PDF or sequence).
- [ ] Renders cleanly on both light and dark YouTube UI backgrounds without disappearing.

### 2. Attention & stopping power (AHQ “A”) (9 gates)
- [ ] At least one proven attention technique is present and dominant: expressive face, high-contrast color block, valued item, number, or unique non-stock photo.
- [ ] If a face is used, the expression is strong and non-neutral (surprise, shock, excitement, concern, intensity) — readable at 120 px; neutral faces fail.
- [ ] Face (when present) occupies ~30–60 % of the frame; eyes sit in the upper third.
- [ ] High internal contrast (subject vs background luminance ≥ strong separation) so the thumbnail pops against YouTube’s white / dark-gray / red UI.
- [ ] Complementary or high-saturation pairs preferred (yellow/black, orange/blue, yellow/purple, red/cyan); low-contrast or monochromatic schemes fail.
- [ ] Color is never the only signal for hierarchy (text or shape also separates the hero).
- [ ] The design passes the 1-second recognition test: a stranger can name the dominant subject and emotion after one second.
- [ ] No stock-photo cliché as the sole hook (handshake, lightbulb, generic growth arrow) unless heavily reworked into a unique visual.
- [ ] The thumbnail stands out from the surrounding niche feed (visual audit against top competitors in the same search/browse context).

### 3. Highlight / Hero (AHQ “H”) (7 gates)
- [ ] Exactly one primary hero (face, object, or graphic) is immediately identifiable in < 1 second — the “1-Second Rule.”
- [ ] The hero is the most remarkable element of the actual video content (visual shorthand for the core topic).
- [ ] Hero is sized and lit so it dominates; secondary elements support, never compete at equal weight.
- [ ] Visual metaphor matches the title’s predicate (e.g., “broken” shows fracture, not absence; “secret” shows redaction/hush, not open display).
- [ ] Lighting creates separation: key + rim or spotlight preferred; flat, even lighting fails the contrast mandate.
- [ ] Background is simplified or darkened so it does not compete with the hero (no busy clutter behind the subject).
- [ ] If no face, the single object is large, high-contrast, and iconic for the topic.

### 4. Curiosity gap & Question (AHQ “Q”) (8 gates)
- [ ] The thumbnail + title pair creates a clear curiosity gap (one of: Question, Wow Factor, Cliffhanger, Knowledge) that the viewer must click to resolve.
- [ ] Text (if any) does **not** repeat any words from the video title — zero overlap; text adds the twist, benefit, or emotional hook.
- [ ] Text is ≤ 5 words (ideally 3–4); longer strings fail mobile legibility.
- [ ] Text is bold, heavy sans-serif (Impact / Bebas / Anton / Montserrat ExtraBold weight), large enough to read at 120–160 px width.
- [ ] Text has sufficient contrast (outline, stroke, or solid color block) so it survives any background.
- [ ] The visual alone already sparks a specific internal question; text amplifies rather than explains.
- [ ] No “clickbait that the video cannot deliver” — the promise on the thumbnail is honest to the content.
- [ ] Passes the cold-viewer test: an unfamiliar person asks a concrete question about the video after seeing the thumbnail.

### 5. Composition & 3-element limit (8 gates)
- [ ] Total focal points ≤ 3 (hero + context/object + text, or fewer). A fourth element is cut.
- [ ] Exactly one key idea is communicated; secondary ideas are deleted.
- [ ] Clear visual hierarchy: Primary (hero) → Secondary (context or text) → Background.
- [ ] Rule-of-thirds placement for the hero preferred (or intentional centered when it serves the emotion); floating unanchored elements fail.
- [ ] Negative space is deliberate; the frame is not filled edge-to-edge with competing detail.
- [ ] Gaze (if face) directs the eye toward the text or object when useful; direct camera contact is also valid for connection.
- [ ] No competing subjects of equal size or visual weight.
- [ ] The composition survives the 120 px squint test: subject, emotion, and text remain legible and ordered.

### 6. Typography & text treatment (7 gates)
- [ ] Maximum 5 words; 3–4 is the elite band.
- [ ] Font is condensed or display bold sans-serif; thin, script, or light weights fail.
- [ ] Letter-spacing is tight on display titles; never loose on body-scale text.
- [ ] Text contrast ≥ 4.5:1 against its immediate background (verified on the final render, not the design file).
- [ ] Outline / stroke / shadow / color chip is present when needed so text never disappears into the image.
- [ ] Text is never the only element; pure-text thumbnails fail unless the niche proves otherwise with data.
- [ ] No more than two type sizes in the text block; hierarchy is clear.

### 7. Color, contrast & brand system (7 gates)
- [ ] Palette uses high-contrast pairs that stand out on both light and dark YouTube backgrounds.
- [ ] Accent / brand color is consistent with the channel’s visual system (one recurring accent across the channel’s thumbnails).
- [ ] No low-saturation, pastel, or muddy mid-tones as dominant colors.
- [ ] Gradients, heavy glows, or soft blurs are used only if they survive compression and still read at mobile size; decorative effects that muddy the hero fail.
- [ ] Body/hero contrast is strong enough that the subject separates even when the image is desaturated.
- [ ] Color is never the sole carrier of meaning (labels, shapes, or text reinforce).
- [ ] The design does not blend into the YouTube red / white / gray chrome.

### 8. Mobile & feed proof (7 gates)
- [ ] Passes the 120 px / squint test: emotion, hero, and text are still readable.
- [ ] Passes the feed-thumbnail test (~160–240 px): the design still stops the scroll.
- [ ] Critical elements stay clear of the duration stamp and any UI overlays.
- [ ] No tiny details or fine lines that vanish at mobile size.
- [ ] The thumbnail works on both phone and desktop feed densities.
- [ ] File is optimized so mobile download does not further degrade type or faces.
- [ ] Cold review on a real phone in the YouTube app (or accurate simulator) confirms legibility and hierarchy.

### 9. Integrity, consistency & anti-patterns (8 gates)
- [ ] No lorem / placeholder / “test” text or visible design-tool artifacts.
- [ ] Visual promise matches the actual video content (no bait-and-switch).
- [ ] Brand fonts, colors, and logo lockup (if used) match the channel kit exactly.
- [ ] No logo-only or pure-brand-mark final image; the thumbnail must sell the specific video.
- [ ] No “Part N of series” or internal series labeling that kills discovery for new viewers.
- [ ] No text wall, no 6+ word blocks, no competing headlines.
- [ ] The design is reusable as a template (structure survives when copy and specific hero are swapped).
- [ ] A/B variants change only one variable at a time when testing (face vs no-face, text vs no-text, color A vs B, etc.).

### 10. Scoring (one gate, pass/fail)
- [ ] **Zero failures across sections 1–9.** Any single unchecked item = the thumbnail does not ship. Fix, re-export, re-run the mobile + squint + cold-viewer tests, repeat until every box is checked.

**How to run it:**  
1. Design at 1280 × 720 (or larger).  
2. Export and immediately view at 120 px width and inside a real YouTube feed mockup on a phone.  
3. Confirm: 1-second hero recognition, 3-element limit, expressive face (if used), ≤ 5 words with zero title overlap, high contrast vs YT UI, curiosity gap present.  
4. Have a second person who has never seen the video look at the thumbnail cold and state the hero + the question it raises.  
5. Only a thumbnail that passes every applicable gate at full size, feed size, and cold review is “all pass.”
